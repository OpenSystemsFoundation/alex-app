# Design Philosophy & Methodology

## Theoretical Foundations

This skill is grounded in a synthesis of design theory, cybernetics, and systems thinking. The C-K-R framework extends Hatchuel & Weil's original C-K Theory by adding Reality space, closing the cybernetic loop.

**Primary Source**: Kroon, J. (2017). *Searching for Hidden Meaning: Developing Insight Into Augmented Reality Innovation*. Master Thesis, University of Amsterdam.

## The C-K-R Framework

```
Concept Space (C)          Knowledge Space (K)         Reality Space (R)
Undecidable propositions   Decidable propositions      The state of the universe
                                                       as it actually exists

        ←── K→C: Disjunction ───
        ─── C→K: Conjunction ──→
                                    ←── R→K: Observation ───
                                    ─── K→R: Action ───────→
```

### Why R-Space Matters

Without R, C-K is a closed ideational system. With R:

- **Observation (R→K)** grounds concepts in reality
- **Action (K→R)** makes design operational
- The full loop becomes cybernetic — self-correcting through feedback

## Cybernetic Foundations

### Second-Order Cybernetics

Design is the action; cybernetics is the explanation (Glanville, 2007). The observer is inside the system being designed. This reflexivity is central to the C-K process.

### Von Foerster's Ethical Imperative

> "Act always so as to increase the number of choices."

This is the philosophical basis for `[O]` Expansive partitions. Design should expand possibility space, not constrain it prematurely.

### Law of Requisite Variety (Ashby)

> "Only variety can absorb variety."

Design situations should generate variety to match environmental complexity. Restrictive partitioning too early reduces adaptive capacity.

### Conversation Theory (Pask)

C↔K expansion operates through conversation — the back-and-forth reaching mutual understanding. Design *is* structured conversation about undecidable propositions.

## Complex Adaptive Systems

Organizations cycle through four phases (Holling, 2001; Abcouwer & Smit, 2015):

1. **Equilibrium** — Stability, efficiency, but increasing rigidity
2. **Crisis** — Traditional methods fail, uncertainty high
3. **New Combinations** — Low connectedness allows novel interpretations
4. **Entrepreneurship** — Testing and selection of innovations

**C-K is the method for Phase 3.** When crisis hits and normal problem-solving fails, structured concept expansion enables new combinations to emerge.

## Design as Cognition

Design is not merely problem-solving — it is a fundamental mode of human cognition (Glanville, 2014). It is:

- A way of being
- A way of observing
- A way of acting in reality

The designer shapes the *space* of possibilities, not the probabilities within a fixed space.

## Forcing as Mathematical Warrant

The partitioning mechanism in C-K Theory is warranted by Paul Cohen's forcing technique from set theory (Cohen, 1963). Forcing is how mathematicians "decide" undecidable propositions — not through proof, but through framework expansion.

This is formalized in the Flypitch project (see `references/flypitch.md`), which provides the theoretical basis for the `[⊥]` Independent status.

**Key insight**: When a proposition is independent of your axioms, no amount of K-search resolves it. The only move is proposing new axioms — a concept-level operation.

## Methodological Principles

### KCP Method (Le Masson, Hatchuel & Weil, 2009)

**K Phase — Knowledge Building**

- Integrate diverse worldviews
- Surface tacit knowledge through discussion
- Identify paradoxes, anomalies, strange cases
- Establish the knowledge frontier (K₀)

**C Phase — Concept Exploration**

- Start with undecidable "moonshot" proposition
- Identify dominant design path through restrictive partitioning
- Expand through `[O]`, `[☄]`, `[¬]`, `[⊥]` partitions
- Let new K suggest new C branches

**P Phase — Propositions for Action**

- Synthesize viable concepts
- Define prototypes, experiments, research programs
- Generate commitment through participation

### Success Metrics

| ΔK | ΔC | Interpretation |
|----|----|----|
| Low | Low | Failure — fixation not overcome |
| Low | High | Crazy concepts identified, but ungrounded |
| High | Low | Knowledge gaps found, but no novel concepts |
| High | High | **Success** — dual expansion achieved |

### Tacit Knowledge Activation

C exploration triggers recall of knowledge that wasn't "relevant" until new concepts emerged. The concept *pulls* tacit knowledge into explicit form.

### Fixation & Overcoming It

Cognitive biases constrain design to dominant paths. C-K overcomes fixation through:

- Formal partitioning operators
- Explicit "crazy concept" permission (`[☄]`)
- Negation of first-level properties (axiom questioning)
- Recognition of independence (`[⊥]`) as trigger for framework change

## Social Construction of Technology

Technology has no intrinsic meaning — meaning is socially constructed through discourse (Bijker, 1987; Pinch & Bijker, 1984). C-K is a *discourse technology* for constructing new meanings through structured conversation.

Technological frames shape thinking and acting. C-K enables reframing by making frame assumptions explicit and questionable.

## The Design Situation

A design situation is a safe-fail environment where:

- Starting conditions are managed
- Boundary conditions create productive constraint
- Wild exploration doesn't push the system into disorder
- New combinations can emerge without fear

The workshop *is* an intervention in Reality space.
